# Phishing Incident Post-Mortem

**Scenario:** A fishy phishing email  
**Date:** 14/09/2026  
**Type:** Coursework / Simulated Incident  
**Course:** NCFE Level 3 Certificate in Cyber Security Practices  
**Unit:** E3 – The Purpose of a Post Mortem Report  
**Status:** Completed

---

## Overview

This post-mortem documents a simulated phishing incident completed as part of my NCFE Level 3 Certificate in Cyber Security Practices.

The purpose of a post-mortem is to look back at an incident, understand what happened, how it was handled and what could be improved to help prevent similar incidents in the future.

This exercise focuses on a phishing email that was reported by a member of staff after appearing to come from the HR department.

---

# 1. Incident Summary

A staff member reported receiving a suspected phishing email.

The email was disguised as a holiday request form from the HR department and contained a link to download a PDF titled **"Holiday request form"**.

The email was investigated to determine whether it presented a security risk.

The email was confirmed as malicious, the sending address was blocked, the malicious email was removed and the affected user account was checked for signs of compromise.

The incident was resolved without evidence of a further threat.

---

# 2. Lead Up

The email was sent by a suspected attacker and was designed to appear to be a legitimate communication from the HR department.

The message presented itself as a holiday request form and asked the recipient to follow a link to download a PDF.

This was an example of social engineering, using a familiar business process to encourage the recipient to interact with the message.

---

# 3. Fault

The phishing email was able to reach the staff member's inbox.

The message contained a suspicious link which could potentially have led to a compromise if the staff member had interacted with it.

---

# 4. Impact

The main risks identified were:

- Malware infection.
- Unauthorised access.

The incident was dealt with before a further breach could occur.

No further threat was identified during the exercise.

---

# 5. Detection

The incident was detected when the staff member recognised the email as suspicious and reported it to the security team.

This demonstrates the importance of staff being able to recognise suspicious communications and report them rather than interacting with them.

---

# 6. Response

The security team investigated the reported email and confirmed that it was malicious.

The following actions were taken:

- The sending address was blocked.
- Staff were reminded to check email addresses carefully.
- The malicious email was removed.
- The affected user's account was checked for signs of compromise.

The response was completed once no further threat was identified.

---

# 7. Recovery

The malicious email was removed from the affected inbox.

The user's account was checked for signs of compromise and no further threat was identified.

The incident was considered resolved once the investigation and account checks had been completed.

# 9. Root Cause Identification

The incident occurred because a phishing email was able to reach a staff member's email inbox.

The attacker used **social engineering** to make the message appear to be a legitimate HR communication and encourage the employee to follow an unsafe link.

### Root Cause

The root cause was the delivery of a malicious phishing email to a staff member, combined with the use of social engineering to encourage interaction with the message.

The incident highlights the importance of both effective email security controls and staff security awareness.

---

# 10. Recurrence

This was the first post-mortem exercise for this scenario, so there was no information available about previous incidents with the same root cause.

Recurrence could therefore not be assessed from the information available.

Future incidents could be reviewed to determine whether the same phishing technique or root cause had appeared previously and whether previous mitigations had been effective.

---

# 11. Lessons Learnt

The incident was dealt with before a further breach could occur.

The main lessons from the incident were:

### Security Awareness

Staff awareness played an important role in detecting the phishing email.

The employee recognised the message as suspicious and reported it rather than interacting with it.

### Email Security

The incident also highlights the importance of effective email security controls to reduce the likelihood of malicious messages reaching users.

### Early Reporting

The email was reported quickly after being identified as suspicious, allowing the security team to investigate and respond.

### Incident Response

The incident demonstrated the value of having a clear response process for investigating, containing and resolving security incidents.

### Continuous Improvement

The information from the incident should be used to improve security awareness, email security controls and future incident response procedures.

---

# 12. What I Learned

This exercise helped me understand the purpose of a post-mortem and how it differs from an incident response plan.

An incident response plan provides instructions for dealing with an incident, while a post-mortem looks back at what happened and asks what can be learned from it.

Creating the timeline was particularly useful because it shows how quickly the incident moved from the initial email being received to identification, reporting, investigation and closure.

The exercise also showed me that a security incident is not only about the technical threat. User awareness, reporting procedures, communication and the organisation's response process all play an important part.
